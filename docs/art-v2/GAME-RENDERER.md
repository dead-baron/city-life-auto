# Art v2 in the game: the v2 world renderer

This is the blueprint for drawing the live game world with the art v2 pipeline (`client/art2/`). It
replaces the v1 Canvas2D world drawing. HUD, input, networking, interiors, tutorial and spectator stay as
they are. Background: `docs/art-v2/SPEC.md` (projection, scale, light), the architecture notes for the v1
client (frame loop in `client/main.js`, `render()` at about lines 1834-2198) and for the world generator
(`shared/map.js`).

## Principles
- **Client only.** Nothing here changes `server/` or `shared/`. Server coordinates are the ground plane,
  exactly as today. `generateCity(seed)` is unchanged.
- **The projection is v1's.** Screen x = X, screen y = Y - Z (world px). One G-buffer texel = one world px.
- **Depth is height.** At any screen pixel, the nearer surface is the one with the larger height Z. This holds
  because screen y = Y - Z and "nearer" means larger Y + Z = screen y + 2Z. So static and moving things are
  composited with a **per-pixel depth test on Z**, in any order: no painter's sort.
  - A person north of a building is hidden by its roof: the roof's Z is larger at those pixels.
  - A car under a highway deck is hidden by the deck. A car on the deck adds the deck height to its Z.
  - **Ties:** a later layer wins on equal Z, so decals beat ground and moving things beat ground.
- **The static world is baked per chunk** (768 x 768 world px, the same as `CHUNK_PX`) in Web Workers, into
  G-buffer chunks. A chunk holds:
  - the ground: roads, sidewalks, water, ground cover;
  - every static sprite whose screen rectangle overlaps the chunk: buildings, trees, props, decks, set pieces.
    Each is composited with the depth rule, so tall things from chunks to the south overhang correctly.
- **Moving things are drawn every frame** as textured quads from G-buffer sprite atlases, depth-tested
  against the static layer and each other. They are vehicles, people, animals, crates, bags, balls,
  projectiles, trains, boats and particles. Their sprites are generated by the same art2 generators in
  workers and cached.
- **Lighting is art2's `Lighter`** (`client/art2/light.js`) in a game mode:
  - it allocates once per size and runs on the composed G-buffer;
  - lights come from the static chunks (lamps, windows, neon) plus moving things (headlights, sirens, fire,
    muzzle flashes);
  - the sky (`skyAt` in `client/render/atmos.js`) drives the presets.
- **Only the new art.** Nothing in the world is ever drawn with v1 art while this renderer runs. A chunk
  not baked yet shows a stand-in (the map drawn simply: roads, blocks, trees); a moving thing whose new sprite isn't
  made yet keeps its last sprite (see "Host scheduling"). The classic art (~250 MB of sheets once decoded)
  isn't even loaded unless something still needs it.
- **Fallback.** No WebGL2, the graphics memory lost three times within three minutes while the page is on
  screen, or `?art=1` brings back the v1 renderer unchanged (and loads its art then).

## Shared formats

**`GBuf`** (`client/art2/gbuf.js`): `w, h, col (RGBA8), nrm (RGBA8, n*0.5+0.5), z (Uint16, height above the
sprite's ground point), emi (RGBA8: rgb + strength), flag (Uint8: 1 ground, 2 water, 4 nocast, 8 wet,
16 char, 32 leaf, 64 glass)`, plus the anchor `ax, ay`: the pixel where the sprite's ground point is. All of
these are transferable typed arrays. Workers return `{w, h, ax, ay, col, nrm, z, emi, flag}` and transfer
the buffers.

**The art pixel** (SPEC "Scale"): the engine draws textures at 1 art pixel = `engine.ap` world px (2; `?artpx=1`
draws full size for comparisons). Workers send art-resolution planes `{w, h, ax, ay, ap, p0, p1, p2}` (packed,
then `downsample2`d unless the provider drew at the art pixel already: `G.ap = 2` from the voxel renders);
chunks are 384 x 384 texels, and their under layer only goes along when the chunk has buildings to fade.
Sprites snap to whole art pixels of the world; the host rounds the camera's anchor (the player) to the same
grid so the player stays steady.

**Height `z` of a sprite pixel** is its true height above the sprite's own ground point. The engine adds:
- the ground height under the thing (for example the deck height for a car on the highway);
- 0 for flat ground.

**`WorldData` (`M`)** is a structured clone of the client's `CityMap` (`S.map`, from `generateCity`), made
once at welcome and posted to each worker. It has the same field names as `shared/map.js`:
- per-tile arrays: `tiles, dist, zone, land, lake, river, distSea, distRiver, terrainCls, bld, ...`;
- lists: `edges, nodes, buildings, roofs, prefabs, handArt, props, lamps, signals, cameras, stalls,
  parking, rail, levels, pillars, gores, walkIns, pois, airports, fields, quarries, raceways, marina,
  ...`.

Workers may import pure helpers from `shared/` (for example `roads.js` `laneOffset`, `levels.js` `edgeZ`)
and anything in `client/art2/`. They may not touch the DOM or WebGL.

## Modules (`client/art2/game/`)

| File | Owner | What |
|---|---|---|
| `engine.js` | GPU | WebGL2 compositor: chunk textures, sprite atlases, depth test, `Lighter` game mode, present to the canvas, quality tiers, memory budget, context loss. |
| `lightgame.js` (or changes to `light.js`) | GPU | Lighter game mode: allocate once, attach external textures, camera offset, cone lights, more lights, tiered shadow march. |
| `host.js` | Integration | The bridge from `main.js`: frame packet, worker pool, chunk scheduling and prefetch, `WorldData` clone, light and sky adapters, fallback switch. |
| `worker.js`, `pool.js` | Integration | Module workers that run recipes: `bakeChunk`, `sprite(kind, args)`. Caches by key inside the worker. |
| `standin.js` | Integration | A chunk's stand-in while its bake is on the way: the map drawn simply on a small canvas. |
| `chunkbake.js` | Integration | `bakeChunk(M, cx, cy, opt)`: ground (from `groundbake.js`) plus static items (from `statics.js`) composited with the depth rule, plus the chunk's static light list. |
| `groundbake.js` | Ground | `bakeGround(M, cx, cy, opt) -> GBuf` (see below). |
| `statics.js` | Statics | `staticItems(M, cx, cy, opt) -> [item]`, `makeStatic(recipe) -> GBuf`, `staticLights(M, cx, cy) -> [light]`. |
| `actors.js` | Actors | Vehicles, animals, crates, bags, balls, projectiles, trains, boats and particle sprites (see below). |
| `peds.js` | Characters | `adaptApp(serverApp) -> art2 app`, `pedKey(...)`, `pedSprite(...) -> GBuf` (see below). |

## Provider contracts (all pure, worker-safe, deterministic, seeded)

### Ground: `groundbake.js`
`bakeGround(M, cx, cy, opt) -> GBuf`. The output is 768 x 768 with `ax = ay = 0`, covering world X in
`[cx*768, cx*768+768)` and screen Y (= world Y for z = 0) in `[cy*768, cy*768+768)`. `opt`:
`{ quality: 0..3, seed }`. It draws everything flat and low:
- **Ground materials** per tile type, district style and wealth, and biome (`terrainCls`): asphalt, worn
  asphalt, sidewalk slabs, plaza pavers, cobbles, brick, lots, grass, lawn, meadow, forest floor, dirt, mud,
  gravel, farm soil, fields, sand, wet sand, desert, rock, dock planks, etc. They must look like
  `docs/art-v2/targets/E1_ground-materials.png`.
- **Roads** from `M.edges` at ground level (`lvl` 0): asphalt band `hw`, lane lines from `laneOffset`,
  centre lines, medians, stop lines, zebras, cul-de-sacs and gores. Also sidewalks with kerbs (kerb `z` 3,
  sidewalk `z` 3), bridges over water (the deck surface with its `z`), crossings and wear. The look follows
  I1, S1, I6 and I7.
- **Water** (`T.WATER`/`T.DEEP`, lakes, river): depth tint, shallows, shore foam, sand and banks, seawalls,
  flagged `F_WATER`. The look follows W1, W2 and E2.
- **Rail track** at ground level and station platforms (flat parts).
- **Low props baked flat:** manholes, drains, litter, cracks, puddle stains, leaves, parking stripes, ground
  decals in lots.
- **Procedural ground cover** by biome and district: grass tufts, flowers, ferns, pebbles, dry grass,
  fallen leaves. These are small upright pixels with their own `z` and `F_LEAF`, densest in rural areas,
  as in E3 and P3.

Ground pixels carry `F_GROUND` (and `F_WET` where rain can wet them). Everything is at most a few px tall.

### Statics: `statics.js`
`staticItems(M, cx, cy, opt) -> [{ key, recipe, x, y, z0 }]` returns every static sprite whose **screen**
rectangle can overlap chunk (cx, cy). That is the item's ground point (x, y) and its sprite extent, so it
includes tall items standing in chunks to the south.
- `key` identifies the sprite (identical keys share one generated sprite).
- `recipe` is plain data, passed to `makeStatic(recipe)`.
- `x, y` is where the sprite's anchor goes (world px).
- `z0` is the ground height under it (0 normally, the deck height for things on decks).

What it covers:
- **Buildings:** `M.buildings` with `M.roofs`, `M.prefabs`, `M.handArt`, kinds, names, signs and doors. They
  become art2 `makeBuilding` specs: style by kind, district style and wealth (B1-B6, D1-D17, I6). Shopfronts
  carry their business names, and lit windows are emissive.
- **Lot dressing:** fences, yards, pools for homes, parking lot furniture, plazas.
- **Props** (`M.props` by kind, all 84 kinds): street furniture (P1, P5), lamps and signal poles (static
  parts), bus stops, billboards, ATMs, phone boxes, hydrants, benches, bins, etc.
- **Trees and plants** (`tree_a`/`tree_b`/palms/shrubs/bushes/flowers/boulders/cactus...): species chosen by
  biome and district from `client/art2/flora.js` (E3, P3, N-series).
- **Elevated highway:** decks and ramps (`M.levels`, `M.pillars`, edges with `lvl` 1 or `'ramp'`) via
  `client/art2/deck.js`, with pillars.
- **Railway structures:** stations, platform canopies, portals.
- **Set pieces:** airports, quarry, raceway, farms, country sites, marina, docks.

`makeStatic(recipe) -> GBuf` builds one static sprite. `staticLights(M, cx, cy) -> [{ x, y, z, r, col:
[0..1 x3], k, kind: 'lamp'|'window'|'neon'|'sign'|'fire', night: 0|1 }]` returns the light sources of the
statics *anchored* in the chunk. `night: 1` means the light only shows when lamps are on.

### Actors: `actors.js`
All sprites are anchored at their ground point. Vehicle headings are quantised to `N` steps (32 by default,
16 on Low and Xbox, 64 on Ultra): heading index `hi` means angle `hi * 2π / N`, with 0 pointing east and
positive angles turning toward south, the same as `e.ra`.
- **Vehicles.** `vehicleKey(d, st, hi, N)`, `vehicleSprite(d, st, hi, N) -> GBuf`.
  - `d` is the spawn descriptor `e.d`: `{m, p, vr, tn}`, the model index into `VEHICLE_BY_INDEX`
    (`shared/vehicles.js`) and the paint into `PAINTS`.
  - `st` is `{wreck, burn, lights, siren, brake, bloody}` from the `VF` flags.
  - `vehicleLights(d) -> { head: [[x,y,z]], tail: [...], siren: [...] }` in local coordinates (heading 0).
  - Models must cover all 26 game models and look like V1-V6.
- **Animals.** `animalKey/animalSprite(kind, pose, dir8, frame)`. Pet kinds come from `d.ar = 'pet:<kind>'`.
- **Small objects.**
  - `crateSprite(tier, label)`
  - `bagSprite(tier)` (tier 0 = cash)
  - `ballSprite(t)`
  - `projSprite(w, hi, N)`
- **Trains.** `trainCarSprite(c, mode, hi, N)`, using `TRAIN_CARS` types in `shared/map.js`.
- **Boats.** Boats are vehicles with `def.kind === 'boat'`.
- **Particles.** `fxSprite(name, frame)` uses `client/art2/fx.js` frames. `FX_FOR_V1` maps v1 particle types
  (1 blood, 2 smoke, 3 fire, 4 sparks, 5 spray, 6 geyser, 8 paper) and decal types to art2 effects.

### Characters: `peds.js`
- `adaptApp(app)`: converts the server appearance `{s, h, hc, t, tc, tc2, l, sh, ht, htc, b, bd, bandana}` to
  the art2 `people.js` app. `d.ar` (archetype) can refine it.
- `pedKey(app, pose, dir8, frame, weapon)` and `pedSprite(app, pose, dir8, frame, weapon) -> GBuf`. `dir8`
  is the v1 order, 0 S, 1 SW, 2 W, 3 NW, 4 N, 5 NE, 6 E, 7 SE; art2's own order is
  `(8 - dir8) % 8`. `weapon` is the weapon index (`WEAPON_BY_INDEX`) and is drawn held.
- **Poses** (v1 `pedPose`): `idle`, `walk0..3` (walk, jog, run, sprint), `punch`, `swing`, `aim`, `carry`
  (also hands up), `fish`, `kneel`, `roll`, `down` (lying hurt), `dead` (lying), `swim` (head and
  shoulders), `ride` (seated on a bike or jet ski), `sit`, `drive`.
- Characters must look like C1-C7 at game scale: an adult is about 42 px tall.

## Frame (engine)
1. **Camera.** Snap to whole texels and keep the fractional offset for the present pass.
2. **View rectangle in world texels**, plus margins:
   - left and top: the shadow reach, about 300;
   - bottom: the tallest sprite, about 600 (tall things whose ground point is south of the view still cover
     it).
3. **Static pass:** draw visible chunk textures into the scene MRT (albedo, normal and flags, emissive,
   height) and write depth = f(Z).
4. **Dynamic pass:** draw actor quads with depth test and depth write. Fragment depth comes from
   sprite z + `z0`. Discard transparent texels.
5. **X-ray:** the controlled player gets a silhouette pass where it is hidden (depth test fails), drawn
   unlit on top. Walk-in interiors are re-baked in cutaway mode when the player is inside (`opt.cutaway =
   buildingIndex`).
   **Building fades:** a baked chunk also carries its "under" layer (the chunk before its buildings) with
   the local number of the building on top in alpha, and `blds` (each building's screen rectangle). The
   host eases whole buildings standing in front of the player (base south of them, picture covering the
   space round them: ±100 px across and 128 up on foot, ±150 and 160 driving) to 0.88 and back (rate 5/s);
   the static pass mixes their texels toward the under layer, and past about half way (dithered) they turn
   into ground, so they stop hiding sprites and stop casting shadows.
6. **Lighter:** lit pass (sun, shadow march, point and cone lights, wet streaks), bloom, final (haze, grade,
   vignette). Present to `#world` at `zoom * DPR` with sharp sampling.
7. **Overlays** (aim line, labels, markers, rain streaks) stay on the transparent `#view` canvas above.

## Quality tiers (graphics presets)

| Preset | Lighting resolution | Shadow march | Lights | Bloom | Chunk cache | Atlas pages | People made at once a frame |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ultra | full | 3 rays, 110 steps | 96 | full | 26 | 10 | 6 ms |
| High | full | 1 ray, 64 steps | 64 | full | 18 | 8 | 4.5 ms |
| Medium | full | 1 ray, 32 steps, no bands | 32 | half | 14 | 6 | 3.5 ms |
| Low / Xbox | half | none (soft contact shade only) | 16 | off | 12 (Xbox 10) | 4 | 2.5 ms |

The chunk cache always grows to fit what the view needs (the host reserves it each frame, up to 32 slots), so
a big screen zoomed out while driving never makes the chunks on screen push each other out.

## Host scheduling (`host.js`)
- **Chunks** (`planBake`, tested in `test/art2.test.js`):
  1. **The view first.** What is on screen, plus the scene's margins (the shadow reach on the sun's side), bakes
     first.
  2. **Then the road ahead.** When moving, the next chunks are every chunk the view will sweep over in the
     next 1-3.5 s (1 s plus 1 s per 320 px/s), ordered by when each comes into view. The camera's velocity is
     the driven vehicle's, or the camera's own smoothed motion on a train, bus or taxi. The view counts at the
     wider zoom it is easing out to.
  3. **Then a ring of 420 px round the view,** never behind you when moving and not at all above 450 px/s.
  - **Results:** in a simulation of a 790 px/s car with phone-like 1.4 s bakes, the old order (ring before
    the road ahead) left unbaked chunks on screen in 80 % of frames, and this order in none.
  - **Uploads:** a few a frame.
- **Stand-ins** (`standin.js`): until its bake lands, a chunk shows the map drawn simply at a quarter
  resolution in about a millisecond: roads with pavements and centre lines, building blocks (the roof raised by
  the height, the south wall with a band per floor), tree canopies, bushes and boulders. Chunks coming into
  view within 1.5 s get theirs drawn ahead, one a frame. (`?art2nobake` shows only stand-ins;
  `tools/art2/standin-preview.html` draws them on their own.)
- **Workers:** four on 8-core devices (phones), up to three elsewhere, one on low memory. A bake yields every
  ~10 ms (`chunkbake.bakeSteps`) and the worker pauses there, so a sprite sent to a worker in the middle of a
  bake is made between its steps instead of after it (a second or more on a phone).
- **Sprites:** a sprite is resident, or (people only, ~2 ms each) made on the main thread at once within the
  tier's budget a frame (the player's own figure always), or asked of the workers. Until it lands the thing
  keeps its last sprite (`_v2k`); a vehicle or train seen for the first time takes the nearest resident
  heading within an eighth of a turn. Nothing falls back to other art.
- **Asking ahead:** everything the server sent within ~520 px of the view has its current sprite asked for
  before it comes on screen; a person who starts walking or turns gets the whole stride; a vehicle that turns
  gets the headings either side; when the workers start, the little sprites everyone sees (particles,
  decals, muzzle flashes, birds, balls) are asked for a few a frame. Fresh uploads count as used, so the atlas
  doesn't push them out before they are drawn.
- **Context loss:** losses while the page is hidden (or within 3 s of coming back) aren't counted; three
  within three minutes give up for the session.

## The living world: wind, plants, water, rain
- **Wind** (`client/render/flora/wind.js`, from the shared world clock, so every player sees the same):
  - 90 s spells: calm 80 %, breezy 13 %, windy 5.5 %, gale 1.5 %; rain brings wind with it.
  - The host passes it to the engine every frame.
- **Plants sway** (engine `STATIC_FS`, per texel of the baked chunks):
  - Leaf texels (`F_LEAF`: foliage, grass tufts, crops) lean with the wind by whole texels, more the higher they
    stand above their ground, so a crown sways over its trunk and grass tips nod. A slow idle sway runs even in
    calm air.
  - A texel looks for the leaf that leans onto it among its neighbours; the tallest wins, as in the depth rule.
  - Gusts are travelling waves that also brighten the leaves they bend: bands of wind roll over a wheat field.
  - Tiers: Low gust shading only (no leaning), Medium leaning up to 2 texels, High and Ultra up to 3. Settings'
    "Wind sway" switch turns it off.
- **Standing wheat** (`groundbake.crops`): rows of straw stalks with golden ears, so fields sway.
- **Water** (lighting, `waterSurf` in `lightgame.js`):
  - Open water brightens a little on two wave trains along the wind, and the sun glints off the crests in
    crisp pixels.
  - The ground bake keeps each texel's distance from the shoreline in the albedo's alpha (`groundbake.surf`:
    191 + px out into the sea or a lake, 191 - px up a beach). From it, a wave runs in every ~7 s (each
    stretch of shore in its own time), breaks into foam, and its swash runs up the sand and back, leaving it wet.
- **Boat wakes:** a moving boat draws its foam under the hull (`actors.wakeSprite`) and drops wake points. Each
  point draws two foam streaks that spread out and fade over 3.6 s (the V behind it), and the churned stern.
- **Rain on the world:** rings spreading on water and puddles, small splashes on wet ground and on anything
  facing up (car roofs), all anchored to the world (`rainMarks`). The old screen-space bursts, which travelled
  with the camera, are only drawn by the classic renderer.
- **Birds in the air** (`o.air`, flag `F_AIR`) fly higher and leave no mirror image in wet ground or water.
- **Wind-blown things,** only in windy spells and gales: leaves where things grow, the odd sheet of paper or
  plastic bag in town, nothing over water, sand or desert. Manhole steam bends over with the wind.
- **Checking it:** `tools/art2/live-test.html` renders the real map at a fixed camera and a controlled clock,
  wind and rain, frame by frame. `tools/art2/hero-corner.html?s=golden|noon|rain|night|blue|storm|fog|incident`
  stages the art targets' crossroads (Holly St x Madison St, Midtown) the way R1-A..E, AT1-A..C and AT2 show it -
  their cars, people, dog and incident - under the game's own sky at that clock, its lights and its rain, framed
  like each concept, for side-by-side comparisons.

## Rollout
1. Done: the new renderer is the default everywhere; `?art=1` forces v1 (troubleshooting only).
2. The Settings toggle "World art: New / Classic" is gone; Settings shows which renderer runs and why.
3. Server, shared and network are untouched throughout.
