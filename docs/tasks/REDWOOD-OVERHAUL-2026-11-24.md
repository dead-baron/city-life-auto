## 1. The tall trees - the proposal
In this view a redwood that is truly tall has its top far off the top of the screen. The concepts (N1-A, N1-B, SC3-A)
never show a top either: trunks run out of the frame, and the forest reads tall by what is at the ground - the width
of the trunk, the buttress roots, bark in long plates, a person small beside it, light coming down in shafts.
Today a giant is drawn 17 to 24 tiles tall with its whole crown: it reads as "the top of a tree" laid over the ground
north of it, and it covers the floor, the paths and whatever is there to find.
Proposed, three parts that work together:
- **The giants are trunks.** A giant redwood is drawn as its trunk to about eight tiles up - wide, flared, fluted,
  burls and fire scars, a few bare stubs - and then fades out upward into shade, with no crown in the walking view. Its
  crown is told by what it does to the ground: a pool of dappled shade round it, light shafts where there is a gap,
  needles and cones on the floor.
- **The tops you do see belong to trees that are not giants.** Second-growth redwoods, firs, maples and dogwood - 6 to
  10 tiles tall, whole crowns - are the forest's visible "trees", set where they frame things, never in front of a
  path, a creek or a find.
- **Where the land lets you look down, you see the canopy.** From a ridge trail or an overlook above a valley, the
  crowns below are drawn as a canopy - the one place a redwood's top is shown, and a reason to climb.
And one rule for everything that grows: **nothing hides what you came for.** Foliage that stands in front of the
player, a trail, a bridge, an animal or a thing to pick fades out near it (the game already cuts buildings away
round a room); a find is never placed where foliage covers it.
This is a new look and not quickly undone, so it is shown first: one valley (round the Giants Loop and Redwood Creek)
built two or three ways, pictures side by side at noon and at night, for the owner's pick.

## 2. The rest, in the order it would be built
1. **Trails and crossings.** Counted from the map first: every bridge over a creek - its angle to the stream, whether
   the tiles under it let you walk across, whether a trail reaches both ends. Then every crossing is one of a small
   kit, square to the water: a log bridge, a plank bridge with a rail, stepping stones, a ford. A lint row keeps them.
   Trails narrowed and worn into the floor (roots, a log step on a slope), with side paths that leave them.
2. **Water.** Creeks with boulders, riffles, small drops and clear pools (W2, N1-A, SC3-A/D); the falls set in rock the
   land has, each with a pool at its foot (four land short of theirs today); banks of fern, moss and gravel.
3. **Ferns and undergrowth.** A fern kit of eight or more kinds at several sizes (sword, lady, maidenhair, bracken,
   deer, chain, fiddleheads, a big clump), sorrel, moss, trillium - laid in drifts by how wet the ground is: thick along
   creeks and in hollows, thin and brown on dry ridges, cleared on paths. Not one carpet.
4. **Things to find, placed by hand rules.** Each kind has a setting you learn to read from the trail: common
   mushrooms on nurse logs and stumps by the path; the valuable and the illegal ones deeper in, at a landmark (a
   hollow trunk, a root cave, a fairy ring in a shaft of light); game trails that lead to the deer meadows; a hidden
   pool up a side creek behind a fallen giant, with the rare fish; a campfire spot on a creek bend or an overlook.
   Always in sight, always with a tell (a blaze, a cairn, a worn side path), rarer the further from a road.
5. **The places.** The walk-in hollow trunk and the root cave (NS1-A), the caverns' mouths, the overlooks.

## 3. Decisions of the owner's this would change (his word needed on the first; the rest follow from it)
- **11-21 15:39, the redwood crowns** ("use C for areas it's covering the paths but in other areas a combo of A or
  B"): under the proposal no giant shows a crown in the walking view. This is the decision the overhaul replaces.
- 11-21, "variety is checked by test" (twelve shapes a tree): kept - the trunks get the variety (girth, flare, scars,
  lean), and the test moves to them.
- 11-24 (this pass), 2,915 redwoods thinned from behind others: superseded if the giants become trunks - trunks
  behind trunks read as depth, not as a wall. To be decided on the variants' pictures.

Nothing else the owner has decided is touched: the greens, the one outline rule, falls as part of the land, no line of
cliff pieces on flat ground, plants lit by the game's sun.

re-bake: it goes with the nature batch, or as its own, on the owner's word.
